Family Law

Divorce Lawyer in Bangladesh

Clear, confidential guidance through every stage of divorce — from serving notice to finalising registration — under the law that applies to your marriage.

Overview

Divorce in Bangladesh is governed by different laws depending on religion: the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961 for Muslim marriages, the Divorce Act 1869 and related provisions for Christian marriages, and separate customary and statutory provisions for Hindu personal law. As your advocate, I identify which framework applies to your marriage and guide you through the correct legal route from the first consultation.

Common Situations We Handle

  • Talaq (divorce) notice under Section 7 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, including notice to the Union Parishad or City Corporation Chairman
  • Khula and Mubaraat (divorce initiated by the wife, or by mutual consent)
  • Dissolution of marriage suits under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939
  • Dower (Kabin/Mohorana) recovery claims
  • Maintenance claims for the wife and children
  • Child custody and guardianship matters
  • Divorce registration and obtaining the divorce certificate

Process & Required Documents

  1. Initial consultation to review your Kabinnama (marriage contract) and circumstances
  2. Drafting and serving the legal notice of talaq/divorce
  3. The mandatory 90-day (3 month) reconciliation/arbitration period, where applicable
  4. Registration of the divorce with the local Nikah Registrar after the notice period concludes
  5. Resolution of ancillary matters — dower, maintenance, custody — either by agreement or through the Family Court
Documents typically needed: original or copy of Kabinnama, NID of both spouses, proof of the notice served, and any relevant correspondence. Bring what you have — I will advise on anything missing at the first meeting.

Timeline

An uncontested divorce with cooperation from both sides is often completed within 3–4 months, largely due to the mandatory notice period. Contested matters involving custody or dower disputes through the Family Court can take longer, depending on the court's schedule.

Can a wife initiate divorce in Bangladesh?
Yes — through Khula, or by exercising a delegated right of divorce (Talaq-e-Tafweez) if provided for in the Kabinnama, or by filing a suit for dissolution of marriage on specified grounds.
Is court appearance always required?
Not for an uncontested talaq notice and registration. Court involvement becomes necessary mainly for contested custody, maintenance, or dower disputes.
What happens to the Kabin money after divorce?
Any unpaid dower (Mohorana) specified in the Kabinnama becomes a debt owed to the wife and can be claimed through negotiation or a Family Court suit.
